# Audio Dropouts in Amplifiers – Troubleshooting Guide

Based on manufacturer and technical documentation, here are the likely causes and fixes:

## Common Causes

**Connection Issues (Most Common)**
- Poor or loose ground connections
- Frayed, loose, or corroded RCA cables causing intermittent signal loss
- Loose speaker wire connections

**Internal Component Failures**
- Amplifier relay or protection circuit engaging due to improper impedance or overheating
- Aging electrolytic capacitors failing to manage dynamic audio signals
- Failing relay contacts

**Thermal Issues**
- Overheating causing protection circuits to cut audio
- Dust buildup in vents restricting airflow

---

## Troubleshooting Steps

1. **Verify all physical connections** – Check that RCA cables (analog audio in/out) and speaker wires are fully seated and not corroded. Inspect for fraying.

2. **Test signal sources** – Connect a consistent single-frequency tone. If it plays without dropout but other sources drop, the issue is upstream of amplification.

3. **Inspect the amplifier visually** – Look for bulging or leaking capacitors and burn marks on the casing, which indicate internal failure.

4. **Test relay contacts** – Use a multimeter on continuity setting to check relay contacts for failure.

5. **Check ventilation** – Clean dust from vents and cooling fans every 3–6 months to prevent thermal cutoffs.

6. **Verify impedance matching** – Confirm speaker impedance matches amplifier specifications to prevent protection circuit activation.
